Canadian-Made Furniture: How to Find Furniture That Is Really Made in Canada

By Modern Furniture Gallery, custom furniture makers in Mississauga since 2000 · November 11, 2026

More Canadians are choosing to buy Canadian, and furniture is one of the easiest places to do it. But a maple-leaf logo or a Canadian store name does not mean a sofa was built here. Here is how to tell, and why custom Canadian-made furniture is often the better buy.

What "Made in Canada" actually means

In Canada, origin claims on products are overseen by the Competition Bureau. Under its guidelines, a product can be called "Made in Canada" when its last substantial transformation happened in Canada and at least 51% of the direct cost of making it was incurred here, and, when some content is imported, the claim should come with a qualifying statement such as "Made in Canada with imported parts". "Product of Canada" is stricter and needs at least 98% Canadian content.

For furniture, the substantial transformation is the real work: building the frame, cutting and shaping the foam, sewing the upholstery and assembling the piece. A store that only unpacks imported furniture and adds a label is not making it in Canada.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Where is it built? Ask for the city and the workshop, not just the country.
  • Can I visit? A real Canadian maker can show you its showroom and finished pieces in person.
  • Can you change the size? Builders can change width, depth and height. Importers usually can only sell what is in the container.
  • How long does it take? Furniture built locally usually takes weeks, not months of overseas production and shipping.
  • Who repairs it later? If the fabric wears out, can the same company reupholster it?

Why custom Canadian-made furniture is worth it

When furniture is built to order close to home, you get to choose everything: the size that fits your room or restaurant, the fabric, leather or vinyl, the colour, the stitching and the legs. You skip import markups, ocean freight and long delays, and you can see and feel the materials before you commit.

It also keeps skilled upholstery and woodworking jobs in Ontario, and it means service is local. A Canadian-made sofa or booth can be reupholstered and repaired by the people who built it, instead of being thrown out.

Canadian-made furniture for restaurants

For restaurants, cafés, bars and lounges, buying Canadian-made seating means booths built to your floor plan and brand, a builder you can call when you open a second location, and reupholstery when the vinyl wears. It is also a story guests and staff can feel good about.
